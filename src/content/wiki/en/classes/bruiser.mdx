## What Bruiser actually does

Bruiser costs 5,000 Cores and it is the melee specialist. You start each run with a **Rusty Sword** and a **Bruiser Chest**, and everything the class gives you pushes toward staying in the fight rather than darting in and out.

It is also the class with the most honest problem: **a 30-player server already has damage.** If you have not bought a drill-power or economy class yet, the [best classes tier list](/classes/best-classes/) explains why those come first.

## Level by level

| Level | Unlock | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5,000 Cores | +50% Melee Damage |
| 2 | Defeat 1,000 enemies with melee, deal 500 damage in one swing | Requires 50% less damage to stagger |
| 3 | Deal 50,000 melee damage, deal 1,500 damage in one swing | Consecutive hits grant +5% Melee Damage each |
| 4 | 5,000,000 m career total depth | Team bonus: +25% Melee Damage for the whole server |

## Stagger resistance is the perk that matters

Level 1's +50% melee damage is straightforward — it makes your sword hit harder. **Level 2 is what makes the class work.**

Stagger interrupts your attacks. A melee class that gets staggered every time an enemy lands a hit spends most of a fight standing still, which is why melee feels weak in games where enemies swarm. Bruiser's Level 2 **halves the damage required to stagger you**, so you keep swinging while goblins and spiders pile on.

That is the difference between a melee build that functions in a Goblin's Arena and one that does not.

## Consecutive hits reward commitment

Level 3 adds **+5% melee damage per consecutive hit**, which stacks while you keep landing blows. It is a snowball perk: the longer you stay engaged, the harder you hit.

It also means Bruiser's ceiling depends on how crowded the room is. Against a single Goblin it does little; inside a **Spider Dungeon** where three enemies are always in range, the stacks build fast and stay built.

The single-swing requirements push the same direction. **500 damage in one swing** for Level 2 and **1,500** for Level 3 mean you need a real weapon — a Rusty Sword will not do it. Look for drops from Goblin Knight (Steel Sword) and Goblin Champion (Champion's Hammer), both of which appear in the [enemies guide](/guides/enemies-and-mutations/).

## Hitting the requirements

- **Level 2**: 1,000 melee kills, 500 damage in one swing
- **Level 3**: 50,000 total melee damage, 1,500 damage in one swing
- **Level 4**: 5,000,000 m of career total depth

The kill counts come naturally. The single-swing numbers are the gates, and they gate on gear rather than on time — which means the honest path is to run the structures that drop better weapons first.

## Where Bruiser fits

Bruiser is a frontline combat class. It shines in enclosed, crowded structures: **Goblin's Arena**, the three **Dungeon levels**, and **Spider Dungeons** — the [structures and dungeons guide](/guides/structures-and-dungeons/) maps where each one spawns.

Buy it after your team's fundamentals are covered. If your runs are ending at the wall, [Driller](/classes/driller/) is the 5,000 Cores that fixes it; if people are dying in rooms, [Tank](/classes/tank/) redirects damage instead of adding to it. And if you are saving for something bigger, [how to get Cores](/guides/get-cores/) lists the fastest sources.
